New York

REBNY

  • No agent name, contact info or links on floor plans, photos or descriptions.
  • The brokerage is responsible for inaccurate photos or media.
  • Never use "Off-Market," and check copy against REBNY's Fair Housing word list.
  • The 2026 rule changes left media untouched.

StreetEasy

  • No name, logo, URL, phone or email on photos or video, including as a watermark.
  • Representative photos must come from the same building and be disclosed.
  • Only legal bedrooms count, and the unit must be shown as it is today.
  • Floor plans: JPG, PNG or GIF, 2MB max.

NY Department of State

  • Ads must be honest and accurate. A November 2025 alert applies this to AI images, with possible license discipline and fines.
  • The brokerage name or logo is required, and "team" must appear in team names.
  • The Fair Housing Notice must be displayed on websites.

NY Attorney General (new developments)

  • Required legend: "THE COMPLETE OFFERING TERMS ARE IN AN OFFERING PLAN AVAILABLE FROM SPONSOR. FILE NO. CD-__."
  • Floor plans must be to scale.
  • Renderings must be labeled and accurate.
  • Common charges must say "projected" or "estimated."

Pending

  • State bills S9584 and A.11635 would require disclosure of staging and AI edits.
  • NYC has announced an AI-disclosure rule for rentals but set no date.

Florida

FREC advertising rule 61J2-10.025

  • The brokerage's licensed name goes on every ad. On web ads it sits above, below or next to the contact info.
  • Nothing false or misleading.

FREC team rule 61J2-10.026

  • Team names can't include Real Estate, Realty, Properties, Group-type entity words (Inc., LLC and so on), Brokers or Agency.
  • The team name can't be larger than the brokerage's name or logo.

BeachesMLS (Palm Beach and Broward), rules updated 4/24/25

  • Every listing needs at least one front-exterior photo.
  • None of these on media: watermarks (including the photographer's), frames, text or overlays, agent, team or broker branding, collages, QR codes or URLs, or people.
  • Virtual staging must be disclosed within the first 180 characters of the public remarks and photo descriptions.
  • Pre-construction listings may use a rendering, with a disclosure if it differs from the actual home.
  • Fines go up to $15,000.

Stellar MLS (Central Florida)

  • No logos, agent photos, contact info, text or graphics on photos, attachments or tours.
  • Floor plans: no branding, but the artist's copyright is allowed.
  • Virtual staging is banned on pre-construction and under-construction listings.
  • Staging can't remove permanent fixtures, change paint, remove power lines or add landscaping.
  • No people in photos.

Condo developers (§718.506)

  • A buyer who relied on false or misleading info in a brochure or ad can cancel the contract or sue the developer.

Square footage

  • The court case Miller v. Sullivan held that the listing agent can be liable for a wrong square footage number. Say where the number came from and whether it's air-conditioned space or total area.

Pending

  • No AI photo bill passed in the 2026 session.

New Jersey

NJ Real Estate Commission (N.J.A.C. 11:5-6.1, amended 8/19/2024)

  • The brokerage name must be larger or more prominent than the agent's name.
  • Nothing false or misleading.
  • Any mention of commission requires "In New Jersey commissions are negotiable."

New developments (planned-development rules, N.J.A.C. 5:26-5)

  • All developer ads must be "accurate, provable, truthful and not misleading."
  • Amenities not built yet must be labeled as proposed, with a completion date.
  • Prices must be the full price.
  • Model units must match what actually gets built.

Out-of-state developments marketed to NJ buyers (N.J.A.C. 11:5-9.14)

  • Renderings must be labeled right next to the image.
  • If the property isn't registered with the NJ Real Estate Commission, the ad must say so and that it's not an offer to NJ residents. This matters when marketing Florida projects to NJ buyers.

GSMLS (North and Central NJ)

  • No watermarks, logos or text overlays on photos.
  • Photos must be of the actual property, with at least one exterior photo.
  • This comes from a secondary source, so confirm with GSMLS.

Pending, bill A4728

  • AI and photo editing couldn't alter listing images, except disclosed virtual staging.
  • Photos couldn't be more than 5 years old.
  • Fines would be $500, then up to $1,000.
  • It cleared committee in June 2026.

One standard that works in all three states

  1. Keep listing photos and floor plans free of logos, contact info, text and watermarks. Save branding for the website, brochures and emails.
  2. Basic edits are fine everywhere: exposure, color, straightening, cropping and sky.
  3. Label every virtually staged photo on the image and in the caption, and keep the original photo.
  4. Never remove permanent features, defects or power lines, never add landscaping, and never change paint.
  5. Label renderings "Artist's Rendering." Keep floor plans to scale, with "approx." square footage and its source. Label bedrooms by the legal definition.
  6. For new developments, add the NY legend, confirm Florida and NJ disclosures with the developer's lawyer, and make every claim provable.
